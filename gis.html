Groundwater mapping in Bangarpet

Research Overview

Bangarpet is a drought-prone, semi-arid town near my city Bengaluru where farming and households depend almost entirely on groundwater. Under the guidance of Dr. Praveena (Project Associate at KSCST Karnataka State Council for Science & Technology), I carried out a GIS-based study that combined field data with spatial analysis to map seasonal shifts in groundwater levels and identify where recharge is most viable.

1) Study area

541.31 km², with static water levels measured at 10 sampling locations during the 2024 pre- and post-monsoon periods.

2) Spatial analysis

Water depth ranged from 5.74 to 77.6 mbgl before the monsoon. Severe depletion was concentrated in the northwest, while the south and east stayed shallower. After the monsoon, a deep pocket of 70 to 85 mbgl persisted in the northwest.

3) Seasonal fluctuation

Some sites recovered by 6 to 7 m, but the central region declined by 10 to 25 m despite the monsoon, a sign of over-extraction outpacing recharge. Overall fluctuation ranged from 0.49 to 25.87 m.

4) Recharge modelling

In ArcGIS, I built a groundwater recharge potential index with a weighted linear combination of eight thematic layers. Slope, geology, and geomorphology carried the most weight (21% each), followed by lineament density (11%), with land use, soil texture, depth to water level, and drainage density at 6 to 7% each. Gentle slopes of 0 to 1° ranked highest for recharge suitability.

Conclusion

The resulting maps pinpoint high-priority recharge zones and support targeted interventions such as tank rejuvenation, desilting, artificial recharge structures, and expanded groundwater monitoring.
